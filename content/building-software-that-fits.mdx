Most failed projects begin with a template and a wishlist. The ones that land start with a quieter question: **what does the team actually do every day?**

When I sit with a client — a hotel, a ministry, a wholesale supplier — I listen for the job, not the buzzwords. Where does information get lost? What gets written twice? What only one person knows how to do?

That map becomes the product.

## Start with the process

Before screens, we sketch the flow. Who starts it? Who approves it? What has to be true before something is “done”? If you cannot answer those in plain language, you are not ready to build.

## Ship in stages people can click

Long builds that disappear for months create surprise at the end. Short releases people can open and sign off keep trust high and scope honest.

## Stay after launch

Training and a proper handover matter as much as the first deploy. Software that the team cannot run without you is unfinished.
